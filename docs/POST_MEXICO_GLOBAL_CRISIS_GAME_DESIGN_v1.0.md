# Argentina Rise - Post-Mexico Global Crisis Game Design v1.0

## Status and authority

This is the master Game Design and Narrative Design document for the second half of Argentina Rise.

It contains no HOI4 code, event IDs, focus definitions, decisions, effects, localisation, or implementation plan. It defines the creative authority from which later design documents and implementation work must derive. A later implementation may never silently redefine its principles.

The post-Mexico campaign begins when Argentina has finished becoming a continental power. From that point forward, the player does not build Argentina in isolation. The player manages the consequences of an Argentina that has entered world politics.

### Core premise

Mexico is not the finale of territorial expansion. It is the end of geopolitical innocence.

The post-Mexico campaign asks one continuous question:

> What kind of world power can Argentina become without becoming a generic superpower fantasy?

The answer must never be only more factories, more units, more resources, or another war. It must be a changing relationship between Argentine power, international recognition, fear, opportunity, legitimacy and restraint.

## 1. Design inheritance

### What is already present in Argentina Rise

The existing project establishes a clear transformation arc:

1. national exhaustion, debt and institutional fragility;
2. recovery through work, energy, production and state capacity;
3. competing political answers to Argentine crisis;
4. continental prestige through the Buenos Aires conference structure;
5. military and diplomatic escalation around Mexico;
6. Mexican integration as the threshold into a new geopolitical scale.

The existing Buenos Aires conference is the most important structural precedent. It makes diplomacy a sequence of tables, proposals, delays, consequences and controlled escalation rather than a single reward. The Mexico chain is the second precedent: political mandate, ultimatum, refusal, war, reconstruction and integration. Both establish a useful rule for the Global Crisis:

> Power must be seen negotiating before it is seen imposing.

### What changes after Mexico

Before Mexico, Argentina is principally the subject of its own national drama. After Mexico, Argentina becomes an object of calculation for others.

The first half asks: can Argentina survive, recover and lead?

The second half asks: can Argentina remain sovereign when every major actor tries to define what its power means?

### Relationship to the existing crisis screenplay

The post-Mexico global opening remains the dramatic threshold already established by the screenplay:

- Buenos Aires recognises the new scale of the country.
- The world names Argentina a continental power.
- The Security Council scene makes Argentina unavoidable rather than celebrated.
- Washington answers first.
- Russia, China and Europe reveal that the same Argentine reality means different things to different powers.

This master document expands the campaign after that threshold. It does not replace the screenplay.

## 2. Permanent design principles

### 2.1 Argentina is a subject, not a prize

Every foreign approach must treat Argentina as an actor with agency. Great powers may court, contain, test, isolate or bargain with it. None may simply hand the player a passive reward because Argentina crossed a power threshold.

### 2.2 Diplomacy is the new terrain

The map remains important, but the central battlefield becomes legitimacy, access, technology, finance, supply, recognition and coalition-building. A diplomatic win must feel as consequential as a military win.

### 2.3 The world reacts differently

No single international response exists. Washington sees hemispheric security; Moscow sees balance; Beijing sees durable predictability; Europe sees law, commerce and alliance pressure; the Global South sees opportunity and warning.

### 2.4 Every gain creates a counterweight

No route can offer only advantages. A closer eastern partnership increases western suspicion. Atlantic accommodation creates distrust elsewhere. Leadership of the South brings expectations, costs and rival claimants. Strategic autonomy preserves freedom but withholds support.

### 2.5 Recognition is more valuable than flattery

The player should not receive endless praise. Respect matters when it changes access, bargaining power, agenda-setting or the ability to prevent an unwanted outcome.

### 2.6 Do not turn geopolitics into a permanent crisis popup

The player needs periods of action between reactions. Global politics should arrive in waves: a summit, a market shock, a security test, a partner proposal, a regional demand. The campaign must breathe.

### 2.7 Reuse Millennium Dawn before inventing

Where Millennium Dawn already models an institution, relationship, market, investment, intelligence action or organisational membership, Argentina Rise should build its narrative around that existing language. New systems are permitted only when the existing vocabulary cannot express the intended experience.

## 3. The player fantasy

### The central fantasy

The player is not simply the commander of a larger Argentina. The player is the architect of a new diplomatic gravity.

The desired recurring sensation is:

- Washington has revised a briefing because of Argentina.
- Moscow has opened a map because of Argentina.
- Beijing has run a forecast because of Argentina.
- European capitals disagree over Argentina.
- Smaller nations ask whether Argentina is a shield, a market, a patron or a danger.

### How agency should feel

The player must constantly make choices with political texture:

- accept visibility or protect room for manoeuvre;
- accept a partner's conditions or keep strategic autonomy;
- answer a provocation publicly or quietly build leverage;
- offer leadership to weaker states or demand discipline from them;
- invest in global credibility or spend it on a regional objective.

The campaign succeeds when the player sometimes chooses restraint because restraint is powerful, not because the game has removed options.

### What this fantasy must not become

- a map-painting extension after Mexico;
- a binary USA-versus-Russia choice with China as a resource dispenser;
- a collection of named alliances that all grant similar bonuses;
- a sequence in which every world actor becomes either a friend or enemy;
- a fantasy of Argentine omnipotence without diplomatic cost.

## 4. World reaction map

The following actor studies are design frames, not predetermined outcomes. Each actor requires multiple plausible reactions governed by the global situation, Argentina's posture and the actor's own commitments.

### United States

**Initial reaction:** a strategic alarm. Argentina's northern frontier and continental scale turn a previously regional actor into a direct hemispheric problem.

**Primary fear:** that a precedent of unilateral continental revision weakens American security, influence and the assumption that the Western Hemisphere can be managed from Washington.

**Primary interest:** prevent the emergence of a hostile Argentine-led order while avoiding a confrontation that drives Argentina permanently into a rival camp.

**Opportunity seen:** negotiated stability, managed trade, intelligence visibility, selective containment through partners, and a chance to define rules before Argentina defines them alone.

**Diplomatic instruments:** recognition or non-recognition, hemispheric dialogue, pressure through partners, security consultations, public statements, bilateral talks, multilateral resolutions and calibrated isolation.

**Economic instruments:** investment access, market pressure, financial confidence, technology restrictions, supply-chain incentives, sanctions only after an unmistakable escalation, and preferential access only after credible detente.

**Military instruments:** exercises, basing diplomacy with partners, maritime presence, readiness signalling and deterrence posture. Direct war must remain an extreme late-game possibility, never the opening answer.

**Intelligence instruments:** monitoring Argentine force posture, partner cultivation, influence campaigns, exposure of covert expansion, counter-intelligence cooperation and assessment of Argentine strategic programmes.

**Evolution over months:** Washington can move from containment to armed prudence, pragmatic accommodation or a colder hemispheric rivalry. The route depends on whether Argentina creates dependencies, restraint or fear.

**Design role:** the United States is the pressure gauge. Its reaction tells the player how seriously the world treats Argentine power, but must not become the only story in the world.

### Russia

**Initial reaction:** cautious opportunity. Moscow sees a potential counterweight in a hemisphere historically shaped by American influence.

**Primary fear:** that Argentina will seek leverage from Russia without accepting any durable relationship, or that Argentine expansion becomes so destabilising that it strengthens the Atlantic position.

**Primary interest:** a sovereign partner that complicates unilateral American influence without becoming a dependent client.

**Opportunity seen:** political dialogue, defence-industrial exchange, energy and nuclear cooperation where the wider world permits it, and symbolic proof that multipolarity has a presence in the Americas.

**Diplomatic instruments:** strategic dialogue, summit diplomacy, public support for sovereignty, joint statements, invitations to broader non-western forums and carefully staged defence contacts.

**Economic instruments:** energy coordination, technology discussions, market access, credit where credible, transport corridors and controlled industrial cooperation.

**Military instruments:** staff dialogue, exercises, procurement dialogue, doctrinal exchange and security consultation. No automatic military alliance should arise from a first opening.

**Intelligence instruments:** sharing assessments of regional pressure, counter-influence cooperation, and monitoring of Atlantic deployments. Intelligence must be an ambiguous tool, useful but never cost-free.

**Evolution over months:** Russia can become a genuine strategic interlocutor, a cautious observer or a partner that demands too much political alignment. The player should be able to welcome Moscow without surrendering Argentine identity.

**Design role:** Russia tests whether the player wants multipolar balance or merely a new patron.

### China

**Initial reaction:** analytical interest rather than ideological excitement. Beijing asks whether Argentina is stable, predictable and large enough to justify a long horizon.

**Primary fear:** that Argentina is a volatile geopolitical wager, a future American battleground, or an unreliable market after rapid expansion.

**Primary interest:** access, predictability, long-term logistics, industrial partnership and a sovereign interlocutor not wholly captured by an opposing bloc.

**Opportunity seen:** ports, infrastructure, energy, food, minerals, manufacturing networks, South-South prestige and a continental market with strategic access to both oceans.

**Diplomatic instruments:** economic commissions, state visits, development dialogues, quiet mediation, regional trade proposals and invitations into broader cooperative networks.

**Economic instruments:** infrastructure finance, industrial projects, supply contracts, energy cooperation, port and rail development, investment packages and market access.

**Military instruments:** limited naval visits, security dialogue, technological contacts and non-escalatory defence cooperation. China should not appear as an instant military saviour.

**Intelligence instruments:** commercial intelligence, supply-chain assessment, technology protection and quiet monitoring of Argentine political reliability.

**Evolution over months:** China can become Argentina's most patient partner, a demanding investor, a cautious observer or a distant power that steps back when the risk of confrontation is too high.

**Design role:** China makes economic sovereignty a geopolitical choice rather than a simple economic buff.

### European Union and European powers

**Initial reaction:** divided concern. Europe reads Argentina through law, markets, Atlantic ties, human consequences and commercial opportunity.

**Primary fear:** that accepting a continental revision without conditions damages international norms, while overreaction sacrifices access and influence to rival powers.

**Primary interest:** stabilise the situation, preserve trade, protect diplomatic relevance and avoid a binary division of the Americas.

**Opportunity seen:** mediation, investment, regulatory dialogue, green-energy cooperation, agricultural and industrial exchange, and a bridge between Argentina and the Atlantic world.

**Diplomatic instruments:** collective statements, member-state initiatives, mediation offers, legal framing, parliamentary and ministerial contacts, observer participation in conferences.

**Economic instruments:** trade arrangements, investment, standards, insurance, green transition projects, credit confidence and selective restrictions.

**Military instruments:** mostly indirect: Atlantic consultation, training, naval visits, export controls and security dialogue. Europe should rarely be the initiator of a military confrontation in this theatre.

**Intelligence instruments:** regulatory intelligence, security cooperation, monitoring of technology transfer, maritime awareness and coordination with Atlantic partners.

**Evolution over months:** Europe can become commercially pragmatic, diplomatically cautious or Atlanticly aligned. Its internal diversity is an advantage for design: Argentina can win particular capitals without claiming to have won Europe as a whole.

**Design role:** Europe is the test of legitimacy. It asks whether Argentine power can become normalised rather than merely feared.

### NATO

**Initial reaction:** strategic attention rather than automatic mobilisation. NATO is not a character; it is a security framework whose members will disagree in emphasis.

**Primary fear:** that Argentine military growth, external partnerships or maritime posture could create an avoidable Atlantic security problem.

**Primary interest:** maintain deterrence credibility and visibility without turning the Americas into an unnecessary frontline.

**Opportunity seen:** deconfliction, maritime transparency, non-escalation understandings and a managed relationship that prevents miscalculation.

**Diplomatic instruments:** consultation, joint statements, partner outreach, security dialogue and visibility through member states.

**Economic instruments:** defence-industrial restrictions, technology access, procurement pressure and financial signalling.

**Military instruments:** exercises, naval presence, readiness displays, surveillance and partner capacity building.

**Intelligence instruments:** strategic assessment, maritime surveillance, counter-proliferation scrutiny and liaison with regional partners.

**Evolution over months:** NATO should become a background strategic weather system. It is felt through American and European choices, not treated as a separate villain with constant popups.

**Design role:** a ceiling on reckless escalation and a source of tension when Argentina seeks external military ties.

### United Nations

**Initial reaction:** procedural scrutiny, legitimacy pressure and a forum in which Argentina cannot simply speak to itself.

**Primary fear:** that a major territorial change becomes a precedent for further revisionism or that the Council becomes unable to describe a visible crisis.

**Primary interest:** de-escalation, humanitarian credibility, legal language, recognition of facts without endorsing future conflict.

**Opportunity seen:** Argentina can demonstrate restraint, invite observation, make commitments, present reconstruction and turn a hostile forum into a stage for responsible power.

**Diplomatic instruments:** emergency sessions, resolutions, recognition, observer invitations, mediation, humanitarian language and multilateral contact.

**Economic instruments:** aid narratives, reconstruction legitimacy, development coordination and reputational influence on investment confidence.

**Military instruments:** political pressure only in the opening act. Peacekeeping or monitoring concepts should remain late and conditional, never automatic.

**Intelligence instruments:** reporting, verification, inspection and information legitimacy rather than espionage fantasy.

**Evolution over months:** the UN can move from alarm to normalisation, remain a source of procedural friction, or become a platform through which Argentina demonstrates responsible leadership.

**Design role:** the UN makes legitimacy playable. It should not be a punishment menu.

### International markets

**Initial reaction:** uncertainty before judgement. Markets do not have one voice; they price stability, access, debt credibility, infrastructure, supply and political risk.

**Primary fear:** that integration creates governance strain, sanctions exposure, supply disruption, debt risk or uncontrolled military spending.

**Primary interest:** predictable rules, transport security, export reliability, currency confidence and profitable access to a larger Argentine economy.

**Opportunity seen:** scale, resources, continental demand, industrial consolidation, energy exports and infrastructure expansion.

**Diplomatic instruments:** none in the classical sense. Markets react to credibility, messaging, agreements, legal predictability and external confidence.

**Economic instruments:** credit conditions, investment, insurance costs, access to capital, supply contracts, commodity demand and currency pressure.

**Military instruments:** none directly; markets instead react to perceived security risk.

**Intelligence instruments:** risk analysis, commercial due diligence, ratings-like signals and scrutiny of strategic sectors.

**Evolution over months:** markets can reward Argentina for stabilising a continental economy, punish overreach, or become a field where different foreign blocs compete to finance Argentine development.

**Design role:** the market is the discipline that prevents global diplomacy from becoming pure theatre.

### Latin America

**Initial reaction:** admiration, fear, calculation and resentment at once. No regional reaction should be uniform.

**Primary fear:** that Argentine leadership is merely a more sophisticated name for Argentine domination.

**Primary interest:** access to Argentina's market, protection from external pressure, infrastructure, energy, political dignity and a stronger regional voice.

**Opportunity seen:** continental bargaining power, South American integration, common development, security coordination, trade and protection from being reduced to great-power proxies.

**Diplomatic instruments:** conferences, bilateral guarantees of respect, regional summits, mediation, development partnerships, cultural diplomacy and institutional participation.

**Economic instruments:** common market projects, transport links, energy grids, agricultural exchange, credit, reconstruction support and regional procurement.

**Military instruments:** training, disaster response, maritime security, peace support and collective deterrence only after political legitimacy has been earned.

**Intelligence instruments:** anti-trafficking cooperation, shared regional security assessment, counter-espionage against outside interference and border intelligence.

**Evolution over months:** Latin America can become Argentina's base of legitimacy, its most demanding constituency or the site of competing regional leadership claims.

**Design role:** this is the moral test of Argentine power. The player must demonstrate whether leadership means partnership.

### Africa

**Initial reaction:** interested observation from states accustomed to being courted by larger powers.

**Primary fear:** that Argentina arrives as another extractor of resources or as a symbolic leader without capacity to deliver.

**Primary interest:** diversified partnerships, South-South diplomacy, food and energy security, industrial development, infrastructure and diplomatic backing in multilateral forums.

**Opportunity seen:** a non-colonial narrative of cooperation, shared development institutions, agricultural and energy expertise, and support for a more plural international order.

**Diplomatic instruments:** summit invitations, development dialogue, multilateral voting coalitions, cultural exchange and mediation on issues where Argentina has credibility.

**Economic instruments:** agricultural technology, energy cooperation, medical and educational exchange, infrastructure co-financing and trade missions.

**Military instruments:** limited training, humanitarian logistics and peace-support cooperation. No Africa-wide military project belongs in the early Global Crisis.

**Intelligence instruments:** maritime security, trafficking awareness, counter-terror cooperation where appropriate and protection of shared projects.

**Evolution over months:** Africa can become the proving ground of Argentine South-South leadership, but only if Argentina offers capacity rather than speeches.

**Design role:** Africa makes the Global South route concrete and prevents it from becoming a slogan.

### Middle East

**Initial reaction:** pragmatic curiosity. The region has experience balancing external patrons, energy interests, security crises and rival claims.

**Primary fear:** that Argentina becomes a proxy in an existing great-power struggle or exports instability into energy and maritime markets.

**Primary interest:** diversified food, energy and industrial relationships; diplomatic support; and another actor capable of resisting simplistic bloc politics.

**Opportunity seen:** energy diplomacy, agricultural security, defence-industrial dialogue, mediation credibility and trade routes.

**Diplomatic instruments:** bilateral state visits, energy forums, mediation offers, neutral venues, food-security agreements and multilateral alignment on sovereignty.

**Economic instruments:** energy supply, agricultural exports, infrastructure, sovereign finance and logistics partnerships.

**Military instruments:** limited defence dialogue, training, maritime security and procurement discussions with strict escalation limits.

**Intelligence instruments:** maritime awareness, counter-terror cooperation, protection of critical infrastructure and assessment of external influence.

**Evolution over months:** the Middle East becomes valuable if Argentina preserves flexibility. A single alignment that makes Argentina a proxy should close more doors than it opens.

**Design role:** a late-game laboratory for pragmatic multipolar diplomacy.

### India

**Initial reaction:** interest in another large democratic or sovereign actor seeking room between established poles.

**Primary fear:** that Argentina's alignment hardens into an exclusive bloc and eliminates the value of an independent South-South relationship.

**Primary interest:** strategic autonomy, technology, trade, food and energy security, reform of global representation and Global South credibility.

**Opportunity seen:** a peer-level partnership that is neither Atlantic dependence nor Chinese subordination.

**Diplomatic instruments:** strategic-autonomy dialogue, summit diplomacy, technology forums, multilateral coordination and South-South representation.

**Economic instruments:** pharmaceuticals, technology, agriculture, energy, services, industrial investment and transport links.

**Military instruments:** limited naval dialogue, defence-industrial exchange and disaster relief cooperation, never an early automatic military axis.

**Intelligence instruments:** cyber-security dialogue, maritime awareness and protection of critical technology.

**Evolution over months:** India is the natural partner of a true non-aligned or multipolar Argentine route. It should become less available if Argentina chooses total dependence on another pole.

**Design role:** India proves that autonomy can be a network rather than isolation.

### ASEAN

**Initial reaction:** commercial attention and strategic caution. ASEAN states will value stability, trade and non-interference more than grand ideological language.

**Primary fear:** that Argentine global ambition adds another disruptive rival to an already contested Pacific order.

**Primary interest:** diversified trade, food security, energy, investment, port access and a partner that does not demand bloc loyalty.

**Opportunity seen:** agricultural exchange, maritime commerce, technology, market diversification and a bridge between Latin America and the Indo-Pacific.

**Diplomatic instruments:** dialogue partnerships, trade missions, ministerial meetings, maritime confidence-building and non-interference language.

**Economic instruments:** food contracts, logistics, port cooperation, industrial supply chains, energy and digital services.

**Military instruments:** maritime safety, humanitarian relief, anti-piracy cooperation and limited exercises. No Indo-Pacific military projection fantasy belongs early in this campaign.

**Intelligence instruments:** maritime security, cyber resilience, anti-trafficking cooperation and supply-chain assessment.

**Evolution over months:** ASEAN becomes a reward for Argentine credibility as a stable trading power, not a territory for ideological competition.

**Design role:** ASEAN anchors the global turn in commerce and restraint.

## 5. The great diplomatic paths

These are not pre-written destinies. They are distinct power fantasies, each with a political cost. The player should enter them gradually through commitments, not select a permanent label in one moment.

### Strategic neutrality

**Promise:** Argentina becomes difficult to coerce because it belongs fully to no camp.

**Advantages:** maximal diplomatic flexibility; access to multiple partners; strong legitimacy among states wary of blocs; reduced risk of becoming a proxy.

**Costs:** fewer guaranteed partners; slower technology and security access; every crisis demands more Argentine effort; partners may doubt reliability.

**Power fantasy:** the indispensable broker, respected because nobody owns it.

**Narrative coherence:** extremely strong. It continues the idea that Argentina survived crisis by refusing both collapse and dependence.

**Gameplay impact:** more negotiation, mediation and balancing; fewer large immediate rewards; broader optionality.

**Balance risk:** cannot become an excuse to collect every benefit from every bloc. Each arrangement must create suspicion somewhere else.

### Buenos Aires - Moscow strategic understanding

**Promise:** Argentina helps make multipolarity visible in the Western Hemisphere.

**Advantages:** strategic prestige; access to dialogue outside the Atlantic frame; stronger deterrent reputation; room for industrial and energy cooperation.

**Costs:** western suspicion; greater scrutiny; pressure to take positions in conflicts that do not directly concern Argentina; risk of being treated as a symbolic outpost.

**Power fantasy:** the southern counterweight that forces Washington to share the map.

**Narrative coherence:** strong only when Argentina remains a partner, not a client.

**Gameplay impact:** security dialogue, diplomatic leverage and high-stakes crises; lower ease with Atlantic or European partners.

**Balance risk:** must not grant automatic alliance, protection or technology. Every gain must require proof that Argentina retains its own agenda.

### Buenos Aires - Beijing development compact

**Promise:** Argentina converts continental scale into long-term industrial and logistical power.

**Advantages:** infrastructure, trade, energy, industry, ports and access to a patient partner.

**Costs:** dependency anxiety; domestic debate over sovereignty; American and European concern; pressure to demonstrate commercial reliability.

**Power fantasy:** the builder of a continental economy too important to ignore.

**Narrative coherence:** strong because it turns the post-Mexico map into a development challenge rather than merely a military trophy.

**Gameplay impact:** long-horizon projects, supply chains, market choices and infrastructure diplomacy.

**Balance risk:** the route must never be free construction. It should create obligations, visibility and contested sectors.

### Multipolar equilibrium

**Promise:** Argentina actively prevents any one power from defining the hemisphere or its future.

**Advantages:** broad diplomatic agency; ability to convene; strong role in mediation; higher prestige as an independent pole.

**Costs:** continuous maintenance; conflicting expectations; no permanent comfort; moments when Argentina must disappoint every partner a little.

**Power fantasy:** the state that makes others negotiate with one another through Buenos Aires.

**Narrative coherence:** this is the richest route for the Global Crisis and the recommended flagship path.

**Gameplay impact:** summit rhythm, rotating partnerships, public crises, coalition management and earned access.

**Balance risk:** complexity. The route needs a small number of visible relationships, not a spreadsheet of every country.

### Leadership of the Global South

**Promise:** Argentina transforms its own rise into a platform for states that want development, dignity and representation without dependence.

**Advantages:** broad legitimacy, multilateral voting strength, moral authority, new markets and a distinctive identity.

**Costs:** material obligations; accusations of hypocrisy; demands for aid and mediation; regional rivals; risk of rhetoric outrunning capacity.

**Power fantasy:** the convenor of countries that refuse to be treated as scenery in someone else's order.

**Narrative coherence:** very strong if grounded in actual trade, development and institutional work rather than anti-western slogans.

**Gameplay impact:** conferences, development missions, trade routes, multilateral bargaining and selective security support.

**Balance risk:** do not make all of Africa, Asia and Latin America automatically loyal. Leadership must be earned repeatedly.

### Atlantic accommodation

**Promise:** Argentina wins recognition as a responsible continental power by reducing fear rather than escalating it.

**Advantages:** market confidence, access to western trade and technology, lower immediate security pressure, European cooperation.

**Costs:** domestic criticism; distrust from Russia and China; reduced freedom in some strategic sectors; risk of appearing to accept an unequal order.

**Power fantasy:** the mature power that forces the Atlantic world to respect it by making partnership more valuable than containment.

**Narrative coherence:** strong only if Argentina negotiates from strength and retains its own voice.

**Gameplay impact:** legitimacy, commerce, standards, dialogue and selective detente.

**Balance risk:** must not erase the Mexican shock or turn the player back into a junior partner.

### A new non-aligned movement

**Promise:** Argentina joins India, selected Latin American, African and Asian partners in building a practical coalition of strategic autonomy.

**Advantages:** widest thematic identity, broad partner pool, strong multilateral role, resilience against single-bloc pressure.

**Costs:** slow consensus, weak automatic protection, ideological diversity, high diplomatic labour and difficult crisis management.

**Power fantasy:** the designer of a third space that is neither neutral passivity nor bloc submission.

**Narrative coherence:** excellent as the mature evolution of multipolar equilibrium, not as an immediate post-Mexico shortcut.

**Gameplay impact:** summit leadership, issue coalitions, rotating partners, development and mediation.

**Balance risk:** should not duplicate the Global South route. The distinction is clear: Global South leadership is Argentina's voice; a new non-aligned movement is a shared institution in which Argentina must compromise.

### Recommended structure

The campaign should preserve five meaningful end states:

1. strategic neutrality;
2. Moscow strategic understanding;
3. Beijing development compact;
4. multipolar equilibrium evolving into a new non-aligned movement;
5. Atlantic accommodation.

Leadership of the Global South should be a cross-cutting legitimacy axis, not an isolated sixth camp. It can strengthen several routes, especially multipolar equilibrium and non-alignment, while challenging Moscow and Beijing routes to prove that Argentine diplomacy is not merely another great-power alignment.

## 6. Crisis rhythm and emotional structure

### Opening: recognition

The first twelve days after integration establish a new geopolitical reality. The player should feel observed, not yet rewarded.

### First month: pressure and invitation

The player receives the first serious offers, warnings, and regional questions. This is the moment for diplomatic consequence, not immediate commitments. The key feeling is: everyone has a price, but every price has a cost.

### Second and third months: proof

Argentina must demonstrate that its rhetoric has administrative, economic and diplomatic substance. Partners ask for reliability; neighbours ask for respect; markets ask for stability. This is where the player earns credibility.

### Middle phase: contest for alignment

The world begins to test Argentina through concrete dilemmas: a trade route, an investment condition, an intelligence revelation, a regional crisis, a multilateral vote, a security request. These should arrive one at a time, with space between them.

### Late phase: institution or isolation

The player either creates durable diplomatic architecture, protects autonomy with credible capacity, accepts a major partnership, or fails to reconcile ambition with legitimacy. The final emotional question is not who conquered more land, but who now trusts Argentina with power.

### Pacing rules

- Never run two world-scale crises simultaneously unless one is the direct consequence of the other.
- Alternate external reaction with Argentine agency.
- After every major summit or confrontation, grant the player a quiet operational window.
- Use news only when the world truly needs to know; use private diplomacy for choices that belong to the player.
- One strong symbolic scene is better than three interchangeable foreign reactions.

### Points requiring emphasis

1. The first UN session: world recognition.
2. The first American strategic answer: hemispheric gravity.
3. The first partner that asks Argentina for something difficult: reciprocal diplomacy.
4. The first time Latin America judges Argentina rather than follows it: legitimacy.
5. The first global crisis Argentina resolves rather than causes: mature leadership.

### Dead zones to avoid

- a long sequence of foreign praise after Mexico;
- repeated summits with no material or narrative change;
- immediate permanent alignment before the player has experienced pressure from multiple directions;
- endless minor decisions that obscure the central global conflict.

## 7. Lasting consequences: the scars of the crisis

A world crisis must leave marks. These are design categories, not a list of mechanical rewards.

### International stature

Argentina becomes visible in a way it cannot fully reverse. Visibility gives access and pressure at once. Future actors treat Argentina as a consequential country even when relations are poor.

### International suspicion

Some governments, markets and neighbours will continue to question Argentine intentions. This must be manageable through conduct, never erased by one ceremonial choice.

### Continental responsibility

Mexico makes Argentine governance continental. The player must feel that regional leadership now has administrative, humanitarian and political obligations.

### New trade geography

The country can use continental scale to become a larger food, energy, industrial and logistics actor. But new routes should require stability, infrastructure, trust and protection.

### New diplomatic venues

Argentina gains access to summit diplomacy, regional tables, global consultations and issue-specific coalitions. These venues are not trophies; they are places where partners demand concessions and where Argentina can shape agendas.

### Security attention

Great powers monitor Argentina more closely. This creates deterrence and risk, but also a reason to build credible transparency, counter-intelligence and maritime awareness.

### Domestic political echo

Global status should alter domestic expectations. Citizens, elites, provinces and institutions debate whether Argentina is becoming secure, overextended, independent, isolated or compromised.

### The three permanent balances

Every later design must ask how it affects:

1. **prestige versus suspicion**;
2. **access versus dependence**;
3. **leadership versus obligation**.

## 8. Millennium Dawn systems to reuse as design language

This section identifies existing MD system families that can carry future content. It does not prescribe technical implementation.

### International organisations and membership

MD already represents international alignment through membership ideas, member collections, faction structures and technology-sharing relationships. The NATO, CSTO, SCO and BRICS families demonstrate that institutions can have entry, exit, membership identity and public recognition.

**Design use:** use these as models for staged institutional belonging. Argentina must never be inserted into an organisation merely as a reward. Entry should be preceded by dialogue, domestic debate, external reaction and a clear loss of freedom somewhere else.

### International recognition and UN legitimacy

MD contains recognition news and UN-membership checks, alongside EU news that treats multilateral visibility as a narrative event.

**Design use:** use the UN as a legitimacy theatre, mediation forum and source of international attention. Do not promise a full Security Council simulation that MD does not visibly provide as a generic reusable system.

**Relevant MD references:**

### Conferences and structured diplomatic tables

Argentina Rise already has its strongest reusable local pattern: the Buenos Aires conference category turns a political meeting into sequential tables, costs, delays, outcomes and escalation choices.

**Design use:** global summits should be few, major and topic-specific. Reuse the idea of visible agenda progression rather than creating a generic diplomacy minigame.

### Relations, recognition and de-escalation

MD uses named opinion relationships, diplomatic relations and staged country-to-country responses. These are suitable for the consequences of trust, suspicion, opening and refusal.

**Design use:** build geopolitical feeling through recognisable relationships and narrative consequences, not invisible power multipliers.

### Investments, debt and development

MD includes country-specific investment categories, international investment variables, bankruptcy pressure and state development choices. Its market systems also provide a language for access, confidence and risk.

**Design use:** foreign partnership must be expressed through negotiated development and conditions, not magical construction gifts. Investment should create dependency questions, domestic debate and political leverage.

MD already distinguishes between normal relations and formal coercive pressure through country-specific sanctions and dynamic economic consequences.

**Design use:** sanctions must be a late, legible escalation, never an automatic answer to Mexican integration. Their value is dramatic only if Argentina has earlier chances to avoid, absorb, counter or politically exploit them.

## 9. Recommended campaign architecture

This is an architecture of acts and recurring content, not a list of implementation objects.

### Act I: The world takes notice

Purpose: establish that Mexico has altered the order of the Americas.

Player experience: pride becomes pressure. Argentina chooses how it presents itself, then learns that presentation cannot fully control interpretation.

Core outputs: international visibility, first American answer, first global debate, future diplomatic horizons.

### Act II: The price of recognition

Purpose: force Argentina to demonstrate that continental power is governable.

Player experience: partners and neighbours ask for proof. A country that can win a war must now show it can manage trade, administration, diplomacy and restraint.

Core outputs: credibility, market confidence or concern, regional legitimacy, first durable partnerships.

### Act III: The courtship of Argentina

Purpose: let different powers offer different futures rather than different versions of the same bonus.

Player experience: being desired is not the same as being free. Every invitation has a condition; every refusal has a consequence.

Core outputs: emerging path commitments, new partners, new doubts, a clearer global reputation.

### Act IV: Argentina sets the agenda

Purpose: reverse the direction of diplomacy. Argentina begins convening rather than only responding.

Player experience: the player becomes a diplomatic centre. Other states attend because they need something Argentina can provide: markets, mediation, legitimacy, logistics, energy, food or a bridge between blocs.

Core outputs: summits, issue coalitions, regional leadership tests, global visibility.

### Act V: The test of power

Purpose: present one or two crises in which Argentina must spend its accumulated credibility.

Player experience: prestige becomes useful only when risk is real. The player learns whether the chosen route created friends, dependents, partners or merely spectators.

Core outputs: durable alignment, strengthened autonomy, damaged legitimacy or a new global institution.

### Act VI: An Argentine order

Purpose: close the second half with a world shaped by Argentine choices, not merely a larger Argentina.

Player experience: retrospective power. The player sees which capitals listen, which fear, which trade, which contest and which ask Buenos Aires to lead.

Core outputs: an identity that can support later endgame content without requiring endless territorial war.

## 10. Content selection rules

Every proposed later content piece must answer all six questions before approval:

1. Which actor is reacting, and why would that actor care?
2. What does Argentina gain, and what new obligation, suspicion or dependency follows?
3. Does the scene create agency or merely narrate a reward?
4. Does Millennium Dawn already model the required institution or economic relationship?
5. Is this a world-scale moment, a regional moment or a private diplomatic moment?
6. What changes in the player's understanding of Argentina after it ends?

Reject the content if it cannot answer at least four of these clearly.

## 11. Narrative tone guide

### Argentina

Confident, controlled, responsible and capable of firmness. Argentina never needs childish nationalism to sound powerful. Its most effective language is precise: sovereignty, responsibility, equality, capacity, reciprocity and consequence.

### Great powers

No caricatures. The United States is not irrationally hostile. Russia is not automatically benevolent. China is not a construction button. Europe is not a single voice. Every power must pursue an intelligible interest and have a credible internal limit.

### Smaller states

They are not scenery. Their agency is the ethical measure of Argentina's rise. They may cooperate, bargain, refuse, delay, demand safeguards or seek another patron.

### Crisis scenes

Use restraint. The strongest scenes are rooms, maps, cables, markets, late-night calls, delegations and silence after a statement. Escalation becomes more credible when it is not performed as spectacle.

## 12. Master evaluation criteria

The second half of Argentina Rise is successful when:

- Mexico feels like a threshold, not a final conquest;
- the player experiences power as attention and responsibility;
- every major route has a distinct emotional and strategic identity;
- Latin America remains morally and politically central even when the campaign becomes global;
- diplomacy creates as much tension as war without replacing gameplay with reading;
- Millennium Dawn systems are used as foundations rather than duplicated poorly;
- no route makes Argentina an automatic subordinate or an automatic hegemon;
- the end state is an Argentine-shaped international order, not simply an Argentine-coloured map.

## Conclusion

The Global Crisis phase is the point at which Argentina Rise can become more than a national rework with a successful war branch. It can become a campaign about what power does to a country that once fought simply to recover.

The player has already proved that Argentina can rise. The second half asks whether Argentina can make others live with that rise, and whether it can do so without losing the reasons it rose in the first place.
